# Pump 7 maintenance guide

## Lubrication system
Low lubricant pressure can starve the bearings and cause rapid temperature rise, vibration, and accelerated wear. Verify reservoir level, oil viscosity, filter restriction, pressure-relief valve operation, and the lubrication pump before restarting the equipment.

## Bearing temperature
An overheating bearing accompanied by low lubricant pressure should be treated as a lubrication fault until proven otherwise. Stop the pump if bearing temperature exceeds the site limit. Allow it to cool, restore lubricant pressure, and inspect the bearing for discoloration or damage before restart.

## Vibration
Vibration may be caused by bearing wear, shaft misalignment, imbalance, looseness, or cavitation. When vibration occurs together with bearing heat and low lubricant pressure, inspect the lubrication system and bearing first. Do not correct alignment until bearing condition and lubrication have been verified.

## Operator response
For simultaneous vibration, bearing overheating, and low lubricant pressure: stop Pump 7, confirm the reading with a local gauge, check oil level and leaks, inspect or replace the filter, verify the relief valve, and inspect the bearing. Record pressure, temperature, and vibration measurements before and after corrective work.

## Restart criteria
Restart only when lubricant pressure is stable within the equipment operating range, bearing temperature is falling or normal, no active leaks are present, and vibration is below the site alarm threshold. Escalate to maintenance engineering if pressure cannot be restored or the bearing shows damage.
